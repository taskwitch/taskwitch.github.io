Introducing My Own Timezone Converter

August 13, 2026

I recently vibe-coded a timezone converter hosted on this site.

Why building this simple app on your own?#

My family wanted to calculate the time in my location, so they need to be aware of timezone differences and winter time (quite intriguing). I recommended them to search online for a timezone converter, but most of the search results are buggy and AI-coded (full of emojis, if you know). I can barely find a solution that is functionally complete (e.g., I can input a time in the future and get the converted time), and accessible in mainland China (they can’t Google).

Does it have any unique features?#

This app is smart and highly customizable. You can change the time/date and the title as you want, and make dedicated links reproducing your configuration. See [the next section](## Should I use it? / How can I use it?) for use cases.

Plus, it’s more aesthetic than many similar ones online. The UI matches the style of this website, and includes an interactive world-map with day/night indicator. It is inspired by the timezone picker when installing many Linux distributions e.g. Ubuntu. Additionally, if the converted time has a date difference as the source time, the app will display a +1 Day / -1 Day badge

Should I use it? / How can I use it?#

Use case #1: Sharing an event#

You are going to start an emergency online meeting from your office based in New York, because the server for your SaaS platform was suddenly down. However, some of the engineering employees are on a work trip in Singapore, and some are on holiday in Hawai’i.

You start a new timezone converter page, rename (by clicking the title “New York Time to UTC Time”) it to “Engineering Emergency Meeting”, and set the information on the page:

  • Date: 2026-08-14
  • Time: 2:00 PM
  • TO Timezone: Anything (say, Singapore. You can input by typing timezone UTC+8/Singapore Changi Airport’s code SIN/city name Singapore)

The app automatically generates a link under the “Share” field under the TO panel, you copy the link and send it along with the Zoom link in your team’s Slack group. After your employees open the link, the app automatically gets their timezones and gives them a conversion.

Use case #2: Sharing a converter#

You are an international student studying in Vancouver, but your family live in Bangkok.

You start a new timezone converter page, rename its title to “Bangkok to Vancouver Timezone Converter”, and copy the link from the browser address tab (not the link in the Share field):

https://taskwitch.github.io/apps/timezone/?sdt=2026-08-14T13%3A58&stz=Asia%2FBangkok&dtz=America%2FVancouver&title=Bangkok+to+Vancouver+Timezone+Converter

To share the converter with your family, such that once they open the link, the app automatically get their local time, you drop the sdt=... argument from the URL, and share the following link (displayed as Bangkok to Vancouver Timezone Converter on most social media platforms) to them.

https://taskwitch.github.io/apps/timezone/?&stz=Asia%2FBangkok&dtz=America%2FVancouver&title=Bangkok+to+Vancouver+Timezone+Converter

Note that as you leave the sdt agrument blank in the link, when you open it, the app gets the current time in stz (Asia/Bangkok) and display the converted time in dtz (America/Vancouver).

Your family can also manually set the time and date on the app, to get the converted time and date in the future or the past. If they want a real-time conversion, they can always come back to your link, then the app will be set back to their current time.

Q & A#

What are the differences between the link from the Share field and that from the browser address bar?#

Tl;dr: the link from the “Share” field will drop the dtz field. (So that the app matches the link recipient’s timezone, and convert the time you set for the event to theirs.)

Why is there such a difference? The link from the browser tab is for reproducing a conversion. If you copy the link and open it on another device, you will get exactly the same configuration. However, the “Share” field is for sharing an event. The recipient doesn’t need a hard-coded destination timezone.
